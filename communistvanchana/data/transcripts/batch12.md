        Distribution of objectionable pamphlets and affixure of
posters are still reported from a number of districts. In
Bellary district some persons were arrested distributing leaf-

instead. On investigation the allegations were found to be
baseless.

        COMMUNISTS.- The Communists celebrated Unity Week from
1-11-1942, organizing processions and meetings. Mr.Mohan Kumara-
mangalm was the chief organizer in Madras and though his
speeches were designed to call for national unity in face of
the menace from the Axis powers he made some intemperate
attacks on the policy of the Government in dealing with the
Congress. In Malabar district a meeting was held where a
Communist speaker made an attack on Subash Chandra Bose and
the Forward Bloc. There are some indications in this district
that the Communists, irritated at Congress propaganda which
accuses them of being suborned by the Government, are trying
to get the Muslim League followers to their side. A secret
meeting of Communists is reported to have been held in Trichi-
nopoly, but nothing is known of their deliberations. Unity
Week was celeborated in certain other Districts also especially
Guntur, Salem and Coimbatore, but as reported already in spite
of Anti-fascist professions, the Communists have done little
tangible to help the war effort. In Trichinopoly they are
obtaining signatures for mass petitions to be sent to the
Viceroy for the release of the congress leaders.

        STUDENTS.- There was a partial strike in the American
Mission and Madura Colleges in Madura on 2-11-1942 to protest
against the arrest of 2 students in connection with the recent
acid-throwing at a Police Inspector. The strike lasted only
one day and everything is quiet now. Two of the ring leaders
in the Madura College have been dismissed.

                                        3, QUEEN VICTORIA ROAD,
                                              NEW DELHI.
Secret and Personal.

                                        the 6th April, 1942.

[ms.] Dear Sir Reginald,

        With reference to our telephone conversation of
a few minutes ago, the name of the individual is Puran
Chand Joshi, and his friends would like the withdrawal
of the warrant to have effect from midnight 8th/9th
April, 1942. Admittedly this is a day earlier than
was mentioned just now, but it should be possible by
then for the order of withdrawal to have reached all
but the remoter parts of India.

        If we make the dates any later, it might not be
possible to arrange the interview at all.

        Puran Chand's friends have given me an assurance
that any surplus period of the withdrawal of the warrant
after the interview has taken place will not be used
for any activity which Puran Chand would {ms. insertion, circled: not} have undertaken
if no such withdrawal had been in existence.

                        Yours sincerely,

                        [signed, illegible]

                Secretary to Sir Stafford Cripps.
