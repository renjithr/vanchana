                        Central Headquarters
                        Communist Party of India.
                             190B, Khetwadi Main Road,
                        Bombay, 4, 8th November 1943.

H.V.R.Iengar,Esq.,
Secy., Home Deptt.,
Govt. of Bombay,
Bombay.

                Re: ELECTRIC POWER CONTROL ORDER: PERMISSION
                    FOR INCREASED CONSUMPTION OF ELECTRICITY
                    FOR THE NEW AGE PRINTING PRESS.

Sir,
        In pursuance of the conversation I had with you, I for-
ward this application for favour of prompt and sympathetic
disposal by you.

        The New Age Printing Press, situated at 190 B, Khetwadi
Main Road is owned by the Communist Party of India.

        It prints the Party's organ, the "People's War" in five
languages, English, Marathi, Gujerati, Hindi and Urdu. It
also prints all the books and pamphlets for the People's
Publishing House, the Communist Party's Publishing concern.

        As you will see all the three, the paper, the Publishing
House and the Press have as their main aim, the rallying of
our people for patriotic defence and support of the war
against the Fascists.

        As the Press is unable to cope with the rush of work
necessitated by the acute economic and political conditions
in our country, we have bought a Lino Machine which is to be
fitted up  by the end of this month.

        I request you to grant us a certificate of Essentiality
to the New Age Printing Press.

        All that we want is:

1. Permission for installation of a 2 B.P.H.Motor in our pre[ss]
2. Permission to have a 20 Amperes Meter in place of the 10
   Amperes meter that we have at present.

As you will see, the increased consumption we have asked
for is very small but it will facilitate bringing out pub-
lications of the kind and nature mentioned above.

        It is true that certain expressions of opinion by us in
our paper is looked upon with disfavour by the Government,
but the biggest single fact remains that the Communist Party
is the only patriotic party in India that has throughout the
year 1942-43, practically done a great deal to turn people
away from attacking our defences. It is the only party that
places a positive, constructive and patriotic policy of
national defence and national unity before the people.

                                                Very

        Dr.Ambedkar's recent press interview in which he suggested that
there are advantages in postponing controversy on constitutional
matters till after the war has attracted a good deal of attention
because of the moderation and restraint with which he expressed his
views. This suggests - what is doubtless well known to the Govern-
ment of India - that no statement is likely to have effect which
is merely denunciatory of the Congress. If a balanced case is
presented without abuse of Congress or its leaders, it is likely,
even in the present mood of the country, to attract the attention
of considerable sections of the intelligentsia.

        Action under the Criminal Law Amendment Act.- Government has
notified the Bardoli Swaraj Ashram and the Vedchhai Ashram, both
in the Surat District, as unlawful associations within the meaning
of section 15 of the Criminal Law Amendment Act.

        Government has also confiscated under section 17E(1) of the
same Act funds belonging to the All India Congress Committee
amounting to Rs.1,984 and to the Bombay Provincial Congress Commit-
tee amounting to Rs.2,954 which were in the custody of certain banks
and the firm of Bachraj Company Limited.

        Conditions of markets.- The expectation that the cotton
market would resume trading after the Diwali has not so far been
fulfilled.

        Communist Activities.- The Communists organised "National
Unity Week" from 1st November 1942 and held several meetings. The
Bombay Girni Kamgar Union (Red Flag) also held about half a dozen
meetings at which the workers were advised to agitate for a
25 per cent. increase in the basic pay, Rs.30/- as dearness allow-
ance and recognition of the Union. All these meetings, however,
drew poor audiences. The Friends of the Soviet Union joined
forces with the Communists and organised an exhibition of Russian
posters during the National Unity Week.
